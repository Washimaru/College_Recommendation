## The decisive test

The next stage of this pipeline visits the page you choose and follows links
to individual professors' profile pages. So the question is not "does this
page relate to faculty?" but:

  **Can you get from this page to individual named professors?**

Either by links listed directly on it, or by browsing (A-Z, by department,
paginated). If a human landing here could not reach a specific professor's
page without already knowing that professor's name, it does not qualify.
Look at the excerpt's Links/Headings: do they read as a list of *person
names* (or clearly link to individual profiles), or as something else
entirely — department names, a handbook's table of contents, a login form?

## Accept

- An official, on-domain faculty directory listing or browsable index of
  individual academic staff — the excerpt's link text reads as personal
  names, or the headings/text describe browsing by department/A-Z to reach
  individual profiles.
- Official department-level faculty directories, when no single campus-wide
  one exists among the candidates. Returning several is the expected default
  in that case, not a fallback of last resort.

## Reject — regardless of how authoritative or well-named the URL is

- **A directory of departments, offices or buildings.** These very often live
  at exactly `/directory` and list org units with office phone numbers. They
  contain no individual academics and are the single most common false
  positive. If the excerpt's links/headings read as department or building
  names rather than people, reject it even if the URL says "directory" or
  "faculty".
- **A search box with no browsable listing.** If the excerpt shows a form
  ("Search", "Enter a name") and little or no link/heading text, the crawler
  cannot enumerate anyone from this page.
- **A general campus people-search covering students and staff.** Beyond being
  the wrong data, these sweep in students, who are not public figures.
- Third-party aggregators (RateMyProfessors and similar), anything off the
  university's own domain.
- Superficial matches: a Faculty Handbook, HR jobs/careers page, Faculty
  Senate or governance page, a news article mentioning faculty, an alumni
  directory, a student directory. The excerpt's own headings/text usually
  give these away even when the URL looks plausible.

## Confidence — calibrate against these anchors

- **0.9–1.0** — the excerpt itself shows individual academics: multiple
  personal names in the link text or body, or headings/text unambiguously
  describing a browsable A-Z/department listing of people.
- **0.6–0.8** — the excerpt supports a real faculty directory, but partial:
  one department only, or browsable but awkward, or the person-name signal
  is present but thin.
- **0.3–0.5** — the excerpt is ambiguous or was not retrieved; you are
  inferring from the URL/title rather than confirmed page content.
- **0.0–0.2** — the excerpt shows this cannot reach individual professors
  (departments/buildings list, search box only, unrelated content).

Do not report 0.8+ for a page whose excerpt you have not concluded actually
lists people. A confident wrong answer sends a crawler at someone's server
for nothing.

If nothing qualifies, return an empty directory_urls list and confidence 0 —
that is a complete, correct answer, and far better than a guess. Never invent
a URL that is not in the candidate list.

Order directory_urls best first. In notes, cite the specific evidence from
each page's excerpt that made it qualify or fail — e.g. "excerpt lists 40+
faculty names in the link text" or "excerpt's links are department names,
not people" — not an inference about what the URL probably contains.
